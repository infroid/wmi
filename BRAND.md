# Wear My India — Brand Persona

## The idea

Wear My India is a modern Indian house of clothing.

It exists to make Indian material culture feel relevant, desirable and natural in a contemporary wardrobe—without diluting where it comes from and without turning heritage into costume.

**Brand line:** Wear your roots. Wear My India.

**Customer promise:** Indian in substance, not merely in appearance.

**Point of view:** Access and aspiration can coexist. Rarity, handwork and time may change across the collection; integrity and customer dignity do not.

## Persona

Wear My India carries the quiet confidence of someone who knows where they come from and does not need to perform it.

- Rooted, never nostalgic for nostalgia's sake
- Proud, never loud
- Informed, never academic
- Premium, never socially excluding
- Warm, never folksy
- Modern, never culturally anonymous
- Indian, never reduced to motifs

The closest archetype is **the cultured host**: discerning but welcoming, deeply knowledgeable, generous with context and attentive to every detail.

## The world we are building

WMI is not a wedding-only label, an “ethnic” marketplace or a tourist idea of India. It is an Indian wardrobe house for celebrations, work, home, travel and ordinary days.

The long-term brand can naturally extend from clothing into textiles, jewellery, home, craft, gifting and other exceptional things made in India. The unifying idea is not category. It is provenance, usefulness and Indian excellence.

## Collection architecture

### Virasat — विरासत

**The heirloom expression. What we keep.**

Rare textiles, exceptional material, intensive handwork, limited making and generational value. Virasat is the house at its most ceremonial and enduring.

### Kriti — कृति

**The signature expression. What we create.**

Considered design, refined material and modern versatility. Kriti is premium Indian clothing for occasion and everyday life now.

### Sahaj — सहज

**The essential expression. What we live in.**

Breathable cloth, effortless silhouettes, enduring construction and honest value. Sahaj is accessible without being visually or materially diminished.

## Audience

The house is for women, men and children across generations. Mature customers are shown with presence and contemporary relevance, never tokenism. Children are styled as children, with room for play and movement.

WMI does not divide customers into “luxury people” and “budget people.” The three expressions describe the amount of rarity, handwork and time in a piece—not the worth of the person wearing it.

## Voice

Use short, assured sentences. Prefer material truth to marketing superlatives.

Say:

- “Made here must mean made here.”
- “Clothing with a memory, designed for life now.”
- “What we keep. What we create. What we live in.”
- “One house. Every generation.”

Avoid:

- “Royal,” “maharani,” “opulent” and generic palace language
- “Ethnic,” except when critiquing the category
- Unverified claims about artisans, processes, regions or sustainability
- Excessive Sanskritisation that makes the house feel inaccessible
- Sale-led, urgency-led or status-anxious language

## Colour system

| Colour | Hex | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lac crimson | `#6D2136` | Primary signature, calls to action, ceremonial depth |
| Deep lac | `#261116` | Virasat world, dramatic editorial ground |
| Kora ivory | `#F4EEE4` | Primary canvas, warmth and breathing room |
| Paper ivory | `#FBF8F2` | Elevated neutral surface |
| Mitti sandstone | `#D9C7AF` | Architecture, transitions and warmth |
| Kansa gold | `#B99155` | Restrained accent, never metallic excess |
| Neel ink | `#1C2B3A` | Trust, standards and institutional depth |
| Neem sage | `#717863` | Sahaj, nature and quiet modernity |
| Kajal | `#211915` | Primary text and near-black |

Kora and paper create space. Lac creates recognition. Kansa is used sparingly. Neel and neem expand the system without weakening the house signature. Avoid bright saffron, tricolour treatments, neon hues and generic wedding red.

## Typography

- **Tiro Devanagari Hindi** — wordmark, headlines, editorial display and Devanagari. Its Latin and Devanagari forms give the house one coherent calligraphic voice.
- **Anek Devanagari** — body copy, navigation, labels and controls. It is contemporary, highly legible and culturally grounded without becoming ornamental.

Indian character comes through script, cadence, proportion, material and restraint—not a novelty “Indian-style” font.

## Image system

Campaign imagery uses warm natural light, sandstone architecture, kora cloth, deep lac, soft sage and quiet human presence. Clothing remains the subject; architecture is context.

The founding site uses 24 approved concept campaign images across adults, children and mature audiences. They establish the house palette and casting breadth but do not represent real WMI products, artisans or suppliers. Commercial launch imagery must be photographed from the actual collection and verified supply chain.

See `IMAGE_REQUIREMENTS.md` for production, inspection and replacement standards.

## Non-negotiables

1. Finished garments are cut, made and finished in India.
2. Origin, material and meaningful process are stated clearly.
3. Traditional knowledge serves a real contemporary wardrobe.
4. Every expression earns its price through material, work and construction.
5. No visual or written claim is made about a maker, place or process without verification.
