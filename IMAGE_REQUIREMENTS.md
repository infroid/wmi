# Wear My India — Image Strategy and Production Requirements

## Purpose

This document defines the visual system for every image used by Wear My India. The website must feel like a modern Indian fashion house: culturally grounded, editorial, quietly luxurious and accessible across price, gender and age.

The current website uses only assets that can survive a premium layout without looking illustrative, synthetic or inconsistent. Generated images that do not meet the requirements below are retained as creative references only and must not be forced into production.

## Visual north star

Every WMI photograph should communicate three things before the viewer reads any copy:

1. **Indian in substance** — textiles, silhouettes, spaces and styling should feel materially Indian rather than decorated with generic motifs.
2. **Contemporary in expression** — the image should belong in a current international fashion campaign, not a costume catalogue or tourism brochure.
3. **Human in value** — dignity, ease, texture and character matter more than status display.

## Brand palette in photography

The photographic grade must harmonise with the website palette:

- Archive lacquer: `#681D2D`
- Deep lacquer: `#2D1118`
- Kesar ivory: `#F4EDE3`
- Paper ivory: `#FBF7F1`
- Burnished brass: `#B88949`
- Sandstone: `#DDCBB3`
- Indigo slate: `#263344`
- Neem sage: `#78806E`

Warm sandstone, ivory, muted maroon, old wood, indigo and natural green may dominate. Bright synthetic magenta, electric blue, neon colour, aggressive orange casts and mixed colour temperatures should be avoided unless a collection deliberately calls for them.

## Universal photography standard

### Lighting

- Natural window light, open shade or controlled soft light.
- Warm-neutral white balance; skin tones must remain believable.
- Preserve fabric texture, weave, embroidery and drape.
- Avoid harsh HDR, crushed black clothing, plastic skin and theatrical spotlights.
- Shadows may be deep, but faces and garment construction must remain readable.

### Composition

- Calm, deliberate and architectural.
- Leave purposeful negative space for copy where required.
- Keep limbs, hands, footwear and garment hems anatomically credible.
- No crowded props, wedding-stage decoration or generic palace excess.
- Backgrounds should support the clothing: lime plaster, carved wood, stone, courtyards, textile studios, quiet contemporary Indian homes and restrained landscapes.

### Styling

- Clothing is the protagonist.
- Jewellery should be restrained unless the collection is explicitly bridal or ceremonial.
- Avoid crowns, thrones, excessive gold props and visual shorthand for “royalty.”
- Footwear, hair and grooming must be coherent with the garment and audience.
- Children must appear age-appropriate, comfortable and natural.
- Mature models must be shown with dignity and contemporary relevance, not as nostalgic symbols.

### Retouching and AI quality control

Reject an image when any of the following is visible:

- incorrect fingers, hands, jewellery, buttons, embroidery or garment closures;
- impossible textile construction or repeated pseudo-patterns;
- waxy skin, inconsistent eye direction or distorted facial symmetry;
- floating objects, warped architecture or broken floor perspective;
- embedded text, logos, collage dividers or catalogue labels;
- excessive depth-of-field blur that destroys garment information;
- a recognisable imitation of a living photographer’s signature style.

## Website image map

### P0 — launch-critical

#### 1. Homepage hero

**Required:** one definitive mixed-generation campaign scene, delivered as separate desktop and mobile crops.

- Cast should communicate the breadth of WMI without feeling like a family portrait.
- Prefer two to four subjects across gender and age, or one exceptional subject whose styling represents the house rather than a single category.
- Garments should sit inside the lacquer, ivory, brass, sandstone, indigo and sage palette.
- Architecture should be recognisably Indian through material and proportion, not excessive ornament.
- Desktop must preserve quiet negative space for the headline and CTA.
- Mobile must keep the subject, face and principal garment fully readable behind stacked copy.

**Current status:** the existing photographic hero is usable as a concept/prototype asset. It should be replaced by a commissioned WMI campaign before commercial launch because it does not show actual WMI merchandise.

#### 2. Virasat collection image

**Purpose:** heirloom luxury, rare material, handwork and permanence.

- 3:4 portrait.
- Adult subject; poised rather than theatrical.
- Deep lacquer, antique gold, ivory, blackened wood or muted jewel tones.
- Visible embroidery, weave, zari or construction must be credible.
- Heritage architecture may be used, but the garment must remain dominant.

**Current status:** usable concept image; replace with actual Virasat product campaign.

#### 3. Kriti collection image

**Purpose:** elevated modern Indian occasion and everyday dressing.

- 3:4 portrait.
- Cleaner setting than Virasat.
- Indigo, sage, sandstone, ivory or controlled maroon.
- Contemporary posture and styling; minimal accessories.
- The final frame should feel premium without suggesting inaccessible ceremony.

**Current status:** usable concept image; replace with actual Kriti product campaign.

#### 4. Sahaj collection image

**Purpose:** ease, material honesty, frequent wear and accessible value.

- 3:4 portrait.
- Natural light, breathable fabric, relaxed movement.
- Courtyard, veranda, home, studio or restrained outdoor context.
- Cotton, linen, khadi and uncomplicated silhouettes should read clearly.
- Never make Sahaj look visually inferior or “cheap.”

**Current status:** usable concept image; replace with actual Sahaj product campaign.

#### 5. Made-in-India craft feature

**Required:** a real process photograph from an identified WMI supply-chain partner.

Preferred subjects include loom preparation, weaving, block printing, embroidery, dye work, cutting, tailoring or finishing. The caption must identify location, process and maker or workshop only when verified.

**Current status:** missing. Do not substitute a fake artisan scene. The website uses a restrained typographic/textile treatment until real documentation exists.

### P1 — audience campaign system

Produce one coherent 4:5 portrait for each audience. All six images must share grade, lens language, background restraint and wardrobe palette.

1. **Women** — contemporary adult womenswear; neither exclusively bridal nor exclusively sari-led.
2. **Men** — modern Indian tailoring, kurta, bandhgala, overshirt or layered separates.
3. **Girls** — age-appropriate movement and expression; refined but never adult-styled.
4. **Boys** — natural posture, credible tailoring and practical footwear.
5. **Women 50+** — contemporary authority, grace and personal style; avoid tokenism and nostalgia.
6. **Men 50+** — contemporary confidence, texture and ease; avoid costume-like regal styling.

**Current generated batches:** several images are useful as composition and casting references. They are not yet a single campaign because architecture, colour grade, garment authenticity and framing vary. Use only individually after hand/fabric/architecture inspection; commission or regenerate the final six as one controlled set.

### P2 — commerce and editorial

- complete product listing and detail photography for every SKU;
- front, back, side, full-length and material-detail views;
- one consistent neutral background system per collection;
- fit information across representative body types;
- verified artisan/process portraits and workshop environments;
- regional craft atlas photography;
- care, material and construction editorial imagery;
- shipping, packaging and gifting imagery.

## Contact sheet and approval workflow

Every new batch must be reviewed as a contact sheet before implementation.

1. Inspect at full resolution for anatomy, fabric, architecture and jewellery errors.
2. Compare all images side by side for palette and contrast consistency.
3. Test the exact website crop at desktop, tablet and mobile widths.
4. Check headline and CTA contrast using the real overlay.
5. Confirm that no asset implies a real WMI product, artisan or location unless verified.
6. Record source, generation prompt or photographer credit and usage rights.
7. Export only approved derivatives; archive masters separately.

## Acceptance checklist

An image is website-ready only when every answer is yes:

- Does it feel like the same fashion house as the other approved images?
- Is the garment construction believable at 200% zoom?
- Are face, hands, feet, jewellery and architecture correct?
- Is the colour grade compatible with the brand palette?
- Does the intended crop retain the subject and garment?
- Is there enough contrast for text without a heavy black overlay?
- Is the subject represented with dignity and contemporary relevance?
- Are rights, credits and provenance recorded?
- Is the file correctly named, sized, compressed and colour-managed?
- Can the brand make every factual implication created by the image?
